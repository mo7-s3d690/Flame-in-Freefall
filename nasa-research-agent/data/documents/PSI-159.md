# Advanced Combustion via Microgravity Experiments / Cool Flames Investigation with Gases (ACME CFI-G)

Cool flames were long known as faint glows in premixed gases, but a burning fuel jet that sits as a cool flame in a diffusion setting is a much newer idea. This investigation tried to hold such a flame steady on a small porous sphere aboard the ISS, using gaseous alkane fuels, so that the low-temperature chemistry could be examined without buoyancy pulling the flame out of shape.

## 2. Research Objectives

Five objectives are listed. The team planned and supported flight testing on the ISS. They aimed to determine the conditions under which cool diffusion flames occur for propane, n-butane, and n-pentane, in normal and inverse configurations, across a broad range of stoichiometric mixture fraction (Zst), temperature (Tad), and fluent flow rate. They intended to back the flight tests with advanced CFD simulations, and to find or build a chemical kinetics mechanism that agrees with both the CFD predictions and the ISS measurements. Finally, they wanted to share the results as widely as possible.

## 3. Experimental Approach

This was a physical flight experiment. Spherical diffusion flame tests were run aboard the ISS, covering several fuels, a range of dilutions, flow rates, and pressures, and both normal and inverse flow directions. Instruments were a broad suite of cameras, radiometers, photomultiplier tubes, and thermocouples. Specifications are not given in the supplied text.

The experimental table lists 180 tests, split across normal and inverse flame files. The raw data folders are organized by fuel and configuration: normal ethane, normal propane, normal n-butane, inverse ethane, inverse propane, and inverse n-butane. The folders for individual tests, such as 21088A1, hold test sheets, flow traces, MFC setpoints, camera configuration files, and image archives.

## 4. Research Hypothesis

The hypotheses are that ISS testing can show, for the first time, five things: steady-state cool diffusion flames on spherical porous burners; cool diffusion flames of propane, n-butane, and n-pentane; cool flames across wide ranges of Zst, Tad, and pressure; inverse cool diffusion flames; and cool flames that go out when fluent flow rate drops but transition to hot diffusion flames when it rises, with possible oscillation between hot and cool states. Together they are meant to improve understanding of cool flame kinetics.

## 7. Key Scientific Insights

**Cool diffusion flames are a young research area.** The source says work on them has grown rapidly since their discovery in 2012, and that their peak temperatures lie between 500 and 1000 K.

**Flow rate may decide hot or cool.** The fifth hypothesis predicts that lowering flow extinguishes a cool flame while raising it triggers a hot flame, possibly with oscillation. The table's mix of self-extinction and flow-termination endings is consistent with a study of that boundary, but no conclusion is given.

**Fuel chain length is a variable.** Moving from ethane in the data through propane, n-butane, and n-pentane in the objectives tests how fuel structure shapes low-temperature reactivity. The supplied folders show ethane, propane, and n-butane data and none for n-pentane.

**Models need flight data.** Matching CFD with a kinetic mechanism against ISS measurements is an explicit goal.

## 8. Applications in Space Exploration

The experiments were done on the ISS, but the source does not tie them to spacecraft safety, habitats, or vehicles. Its framing is about terrestrial engine design, so space exploration applications are not supported by the supplied text.

## 9. Earth Benefits

The source notes that combustion supplies 86% of world energy and a higher share of transportation energy, that transportation engines typically reach thermal efficiencies of 38% or lower, and that ultra-lean combustion might raise this to 60%. It says limited understanding of cool flame chemistry is holding that progress back, and that these measurements should support more efficient internal combustion engines.
